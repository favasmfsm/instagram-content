# The Sales Falcon — 20 Carousel Scripts

Nov 14, 2026 · @Gooseberry

## How to use this doc

Each of the 20 carousels follows the same 7-slide pattern, written for sales heads and managers in India. Edit any line inline or leave a comment; designs start once the copy is approved.

1. **Cover:** the hook, in the team's own words
2. **The problem:** what's really going wrong
3. **Fix 1**
4. **Fix 2**
5. **Fix 3**
6. **Try this:** a concrete example, script or rule
7. **Takeaway + CTA:** one line to remember, then save / share / follow @thesalesfalcon

Headlines are kept to about 8 words and body lines to about 25, so they fit a 1080×1350 slide. Each topic ends with a ready-to-post caption.

## 1. Too few qualified leads

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | You hit the lead target. So why is the pipeline empty? | 500 leads. 12 real buyers. Here's what's going wrong. |
| 2 Problem | You're counting leads, not buyers | When the target is volume, marketing fills the form with anyone who'll click. Sales pays for it in wasted calls. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Write down who you actually sell to | Industry, company size, role, and the trigger that makes them buy now. If it isn't written, nobody can target it. |
| 4 Fix 2 | Change the target, not the effort | Measure meetings booked or sales-accepted leads, not form fills. Teams chase what you count. |
| 5 Fix 3 | Add one filter question | Ask budget, timeline or team size on the form. Fewer leads come in, but more of them are worth a call. |
| 6 Try this | Audit your last 50 closed deals | What did the buyers have in common? That pattern is your real ideal customer, not the one in the pitch deck. |
| 7 Takeaway | 50 right leads beat 500 random ones | Save this for your next target-setting meeting. Follow @thesalesfalcon for more. |

**Caption:** Hitting the lead number feels great until sales starts calling. If your team is drowning in leads but starving for buyers, the problem is usually what you're counting. Here are 3 fixes to try. Which one would help your team most? 👇 #salesleadership #b2bsales #leadgeneration #salesindia

## 2. Outreach gets ignored

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | 200 emails. 80 calls. Zero replies. | It's not the volume. It's the message. |
| 2 Problem | Your outreach is about you | "We are a leading provider of…" Buyers delete that in two seconds. They only read what's about their problem. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Open with their world | Mention something real: a hiring post, a new branch, a funding round. It proves this isn't a mass blast. |
| 4 Fix 2 | One message, one ask | Not a demo, a deck and a call. Ask one small question they can answer in a line. |
| 5 Fix 3 | Mix the channels | Email, call, LinkedIn, WhatsApp where it's welcome. Plenty of replies only come after a few touches. |
| 6 Try this | The 3-line message | Line 1: what you noticed about them. Line 2: the problem it usually brings. Line 3: "Worth a 10-minute chat?" |
| 7 Takeaway | Relevance gets replies. Volume gets ignored. | Share this with the rep who's sending 200 emails a day. |

**Caption:** If nobody's replying, sending more won't fix it. Buyers reply to messages that are clearly about them. Try the 3-line message on slide 6 this week and tell us how it goes. #coldoutreach #salesprospecting #b2bsales #salesindia

## 3. Interested buyers go quiet

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | "Send me a proposal." Then… silence. | Why ready-sounding buyers disappear, and how to stop it. |
| 2 Problem | The proposal was the only next step | Once it's sent, the buyer has nothing to do with you. And inside their company, nobody is selling it for you. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Never send it cold | Walk them through it on a call. A proposal read alone gets judged on price. |

| 5 Fix 3 | Find out who else decides | Ask: "Who else needs to see this before you say yes?" Silence often means someone you've never met said no. |

| 7 Takeaway | A proposal is not a next step | Save this before your next proposal goes out. |

**Caption:** Ghosting after a proposal usually starts before you hit send. Lock the next meeting, meet the other decision-makers, and never let a proposal travel alone. What's the longest a buyer has gone quiet on you? #salesfollowup #b2bsales #closingdeals #salesindia

## 4. Sales and marketing disagree on lead quality

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | "These leads are qualified." "No, they're not." | Sound familiar? The fight isn't about leads. It's about definitions. |
| 2 Problem | Two teams, two meanings of "qualified" | Marketing means "filled the form". Sales means "ready to talk budget". Both are right, and both are frustrated. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Agree on one written definition | Sit together for one hour. Write down what a sales-ready lead must have. Both heads sign off. |
| 4 Fix 2 | Add a stage in between | Marketing-qualified, then sales-accepted. Sales must accept or reject each lead within 48 hours, with a reason. |
| 5 Fix 3 | Share one number | Give both teams a target on revenue from marketing leads, not just lead count. Shared goals end the blame game. |
| 6 Try this | The weekly 15-minute review | Pick 10 recent leads. Sales says yes or no and why. Marketing adjusts targeting the same week. |
| 7 Takeaway | Define it together, or fight about it forever | Tag your marketing (or sales) counterpart. |

**Caption:** Most sales vs marketing fights come down to one word: qualified. Write the definition together, add a handover stage, and share a revenue goal. Does your team have a written definition today? #salesandmarketing #revops #b2bsales #salesindia

## 5. The offer sounds like every competitor's

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | Three vendors. Same pitch. Lowest price wins. | If buyers can't tell you apart, they'll compare you on the only thing left. |
| 2 Problem | "Quality, service, experience" says nothing | Every competitor claims the same words. To a buyer, they cancel each other out. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Pick the buyer you serve best | You can't be different for everyone. Be clearly the best for one type of customer. |
| 4 Fix 2 | Name the problem you solve better | Not features. The specific pain where your customers say, "Nobody else fixed this." |
| 5 Fix 3 | Show it, don't claim it | A before-and-after, a number, a customer's own words. Proof is what makes different believable. |
| 6 Try this | The "only we" test | Finish this line: "We're the only ones who…" If a competitor could say it too, keep going. |
| 7 Takeaway | Be different, or be cheaper | Share this with whoever writes your sales pitch. |

**Caption:** When every pitch sounds the same, price becomes the deciding factor. Pick your buyer, name the problem you solve better, and prove it. Can you finish the line on slide 6? #salespitch #positioning #b2bsales #salesindia

## 6. Content gets attention but few enquiries

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | 30,000 views. 0 enquiries. | Reach is not revenue. Here's how to close the gap. |
| 2 Problem | You're making content for the algorithm | Viral topics bring viewers who'll never buy. Your buyers scroll past because it isn't about their problem. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Write for 100 buyers, not 100,000 viewers | Pick topics only your ideal customer cares about. Smaller reach, far better audience. |
| 4 Fix 2 | Give every post a next step | A comment keyword, a DM prompt, a link to a free checklist. If there's no next step, nobody takes one. |
| 5 Fix 3 | Track enquiries, not likes | Ask every new lead: "Where did you first hear about us?" Then make more of what they name. |
| 6 Try this | The 70/30 rule | 70% of posts answer real buyer questions. 30% build reach. Check your last 10 posts against it. |
| 7 Takeaway | Views feel good. Enquiries pay salaries. | Save this before you plan next month's content. |

**Caption:** A post can go viral and still bring zero business. Write for your buyers, give every post a next step, and measure enquiries instead of likes. What's your best-performing post ever brought in? #contentmarketing #b2bmarketing #leadgeneration #salesindia

## 7. Follow-ups slip through the cracks

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | "We said we'd call them back." Who did? | Nobody. And the buyer noticed. |
| 2 Problem | Follow-ups live in people's heads | Notebooks, sticky notes, a WhatsApp chat. When a rep is busy or on leave, the promise disappears. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Every lead has one owner | Not "the team". One name. If two people own it, nobody does. |
| 4 Fix 2 | No call ends without a dated task | Before moving to the next lead, log the next action and the date. Takes 30 seconds. |
| 5 Fix 3 | Review overdue tasks daily | A 10-minute stand-up: what's overdue, who's on it, by when. Overdue lists shrink fast when someone looks. |
| 6 Try this | The 24-hour rule | Any promise made to a buyer gets done, or updated, within 24 hours. Even "still working on it" counts. |
| 7 Takeaway | Buyers remember the promise you forgot | Share this with your team before Monday's meeting. |

**Caption:** Lost deals often aren't lost to competitors. They're lost to a follow-up nobody owned. One owner, one dated next step, one daily check. Which of these does your team skip? #salesprocess #salesmanagement #followup #salesindia

## 8. The decision-maker is hard to reach

| 7 Takeaway | No access to the decider = no forecast | Save this for your next deal review. |

**Caption:** A happy contact is a great start, but the deal lives or dies with the person who signs. Ask who decides early, help your champion bring you in, and give them something to carry upstairs. #enterprisesales #b2bsales #salesleadership #salesindia

## 9. It's hard to tell which campaigns drive revenue

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | You know which campaign got leads. Which one got paid? | If you can't answer that, you're budgeting blind. |
| 2 Problem | The trail breaks at the handover | Marketing tracks the lead. Sales tracks the deal. Nobody connects the two, so revenue has no source. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Tag every lead with a source | Campaign, channel, date. Make it a required field. No source, no entry. |
| 4 Fix 2 | Keep the tag all the way to invoice | The source must stay on the deal when it's won. Check 10 won deals this month: how many still have it? |
| 5 Fix 3 | Report revenue by campaign monthly | Not leads per campaign. Rupees per campaign. Then move budget to what actually pays. |
| 6 Try this | Ask the customer | On every won deal: "What made you reach out to us?" Their answer often beats any tracking setup. |
| 7 Takeaway | Leads are a guess. Revenue is the answer. | Share this with your marketing head. |

**Caption:** Lead counts tell you what got attention. Revenue by source tells you what to fund. Tag every lead, keep the tag through to the invoice, and ask customers directly. Can your team name the campaign behind last month's biggest deal? #marketingattribution #revops #b2bmarketing #salesindia

## 10. The pipeline swings from month to month

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | Great month. Empty next month. Repeat. | The feast-and-famine pipeline, and how to break it. |
| 2 Problem | Closing and prospecting don't happen together | At month-end, everyone closes. Nobody prospects. So the next month starts with nothing to work on. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Block prospecting time, every week | Same slot, every week, even in closing week. Treat it like a client meeting you can't move. |
| 4 Fix 2 | Set a weekly pipeline target | Not just a monthly sales target. A number of new qualified opportunities added each week. |
| 5 Fix 3 | Know your coverage ratio | If you close 1 in 4, you need 4x your target in pipeline. Check it on the 1st, not the 30th. |
| 6 Try this | The Friday pipeline check | Every Friday: new deals added this week vs target. Short? Next week's first two hours go to prospecting. |
| 7 Takeaway | Next month's sales are made this month | Save this and set your weekly target today. |

**Caption:** If your numbers look like a roller coaster, the problem isn't closing. It's that prospecting stops whenever closing starts. Protect weekly prospecting time and track new pipeline every Friday. #salespipeline #salesmanagement #prospecting #salesindia

## 11. Ad costs rise while sales stay flat

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | More ad spend. Same number of customers. | Where's the extra money going? |
| 2 Problem | You're paying for clicks, not customers | Ads optimised for leads bring cheap leads. Cheap leads rarely buy, so cost per customer quietly climbs. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Measure cost per customer | Total ad spend ÷ new paying customers. That's the only ad number your CFO cares about. |
| 4 Fix 2 | Feed sales results back to the ads | Tell the ad platform which leads became customers. It learns to find more like them. |
| 5 Fix 3 | Fix the follow-up before raising budget | Paid leads that wait a day to hear back go cold. More budget only buys more leads to waste. |
| 6 Try this | The 30-day cut test | Pause your weakest campaign for 30 days. If sales don't drop, that money was never working. |
| 7 Takeaway | Spend more only on what already converts | Share this with whoever signs off on ad budgets. |

**Caption:** Rising ad costs aren't always a platform problem. Often it's ads chasing the wrong leads, or good leads waiting too long to hear from sales. Track cost per customer and fix follow-up before you increase spend. #performancemarketing #adspend #b2bmarketing #salesindia

## 12. Prospects expect a discount to close

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | "What's the best price you can give me?" | Every deal. Every time. Here's why, and what to say. |
| 2 Problem | You taught them to ask | If discounts come easily at month-end, buyers learn to wait for them. Price becomes the whole conversation. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Sell the cost of the problem first | When the buyer knows what the problem costs them each month, your price looks small next to it. |
| 4 Fix 2 | Never give, always trade | A lower price for something in return: a longer contract, upfront payment, a case study, a quicker sign-off. |
| 5 Fix 3 | Set discount rules for the team | Who can approve what, and up to how much. Reps who can't discount alone stop offering it first. |
| 6 Try this | The answer to "best price?" | "This is our best price for this scope. If budget is the issue, let's look at what we can adjust in the scope." |
| 7 Takeaway | Discount without a trade = margin given away | Save this for the next time a buyer asks for "best price". |

**Caption:** "Best price?" isn't a closing signal. It's a habit buyers learn when discounts come easily. Anchor on the cost of the problem, trade instead of give, and set clear rules for your team. #negotiation #pricing #b2bsales #salesindia

## 13. Leads book demos and fail to show up

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | 6 demos booked. 2 showed up. | No-shows aren't bad luck. They're a process gap. |
| 2 Problem | Booking a slot isn't a commitment | A calendar click takes 5 seconds. If the buyer has no reason to show up, something else wins that hour. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Book it on a live call, not a link | A person who agreed in conversation shows up more often than one who clicked a link. |
| 4 Fix 2 | Give them a reason to attend | "I'll show you how to cut your follow-up time using your own numbers." Specific outcomes get attendance. |
| 5 Fix 3 | Remind them where they reply | A day-before and an hour-before reminder on WhatsApp or email, from the rep, not a bot. Ask them to confirm. |
| 6 Try this | The pre-demo question | Send one question the day before: "What's the one thing you want to see?" Buyers who answer are far more likely to attend. |
| 7 Takeaway | Booked isn't committed. Confirmed is. | Share this with your SDR team. |

**Caption:** A calendar invite isn't a promise. Book demos in conversation, give a clear reason to attend, and ask buyers to confirm the day before. What's your team's show-up rate right now? #demos #salesdevelopment #b2bsales #salesindia

## 16. Buyers need proof the team can't easily share

| 7 Takeaway | Claims get doubted. Proof gets forwarded. | Save this and start your proof library this month. |

**Caption:** Buyers trust customers more than they trust your pitch. Capture wins while they're fresh, sort them by industry, and keep them short enough to forward. How many industry case studies could your team send today? #casestudies #socialproof #b2bsales #salesindia

## 17. Deals keep slipping into next month

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | "Closing next week." For 6 weeks. | If every deal slips, your forecast is fiction. |
| 2 Problem | The close date is the rep's hope, not the buyer's plan | Nobody asked what has to happen on the buyer's side before they can sign. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Build a close plan with the buyer | List every step: approval, legal, PO, payment. Who owns each, and by when. Agree on it together. |
| 4 Fix 2 | Find the reason to act now | No deadline on their side means no urgency. Ask: "What happens if this waits 3 months?" |
| 5 Fix 3 | Set a slip rule | A deal that slips twice gets reviewed with the manager. Either the plan changes or the deal leaves the forecast. |
| 6 Try this | The reverse timeline question | "If you want this live by 1 March, when would you need to sign? What needs to happen before that?" |
| 7 Takeaway | No close plan = no close date | Share this before your next forecast call. |

**Caption:** Slipping deals usually aren't about the buyer stalling. They're about a close date nobody on the buyer's side agreed to. Build the close plan together, find a reason to act now, and review any deal that slips twice. #salesforecasting #closingdeals #salesmanagement #salesindia

## 18. Teams can't tell why they lose deals

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | CRM says "Lost on price". Is that true? | Often, price is just the easiest answer to type. |
| 2 Problem | Nobody asks the buyer | Reps guess the reason, pick a dropdown, and move on. The real reason never reaches anyone who can fix it. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Call every lost deal above a set value | A 10-minute call, ideally by someone other than the rep. Buyers are more honest with a neutral person. |

| 7 Takeaway | Every lost deal is free research | Save this for your next lost-deal review. |

**Caption:** "Lost on price" is often a guess, not a reason. Ask the buyer, ask better questions, and review losses as a team every month. When did your team last call a buyer who said no? #winlossanalysis #salesstrategy #b2bsales #salesindia

## 20. Sales wins depend on one top performer

| Slide | Headline | Body |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Cover | Your best rep keeps closing. Nobody knows how. | What happens to your numbers if they leave? |
| 2 Problem | Talent isn't a process | When results depend on one person's instincts, you can't scale them, train them or replace them. |
| 3 Fix 1 | Watch what they actually do | Sit in on their calls. Note the questions they ask, how they handle objections, how they follow up. |
| 4 Fix 2 | Write it down as a playbook | Their discovery questions, objection answers, follow-up rhythm. Simple enough for a new rep to use in week 1. |
| 5 Fix 3 | Make teaching part of their role | Let the top rep run a 30-minute session every fortnight. Recognise it, so sharing feels like a promotion, not extra work. |
| 6 Try this | Record and review | With buyer permission, record a few of their best calls. Review one with the team each week: what worked, and why. |
| 7 Takeaway | One star is a risk. A playbook is a team. | Tag the top performer on your team. |

**Caption:** A star rep is great until they're on leave, or leave for good. Study what they do, turn it into a playbook, and make teaching part of their job. Could a new hire sell like your best rep in 90 days? #salesplaybook #salescoaching #salesleadership #salesindia
