Why it happens

They're comparing, not leaving.

A parent who mentions another institute is still on the phone with you. They want help deciding.

Your job is to make the comparison fair, not to win an argument.

EdTech Sales Master03 / 08
First reply

Defend vs get curious

Defend

"Their faculty isn't good."

"Okay, we can match that price."

Get curious

"That's a good option to look at. What did you like about them?"

"What matters most to you in choosing?"

Compare on what matters

  1. 01

    Batch size

    How many students per teacher?

  2. 02

    Doubt support

    Who answers doubts, and how fast?

  3. 03

    Tests and feedback

    How often, and does someone explain the mistakes?

  4. 04

    Similar students' results

    Students who started at the same level.

How to say it

Make the comparison fair

Parent"XYZ Classes is ₹15,000 cheaper."
You"That's a real difference. Before you decide, ask them three things: batch size, who clears doubts, and how often tests are reviewed. Then compare us on the same three."
Parent"Okay. I hadn't asked them that."
WhyYou're helping them choose well. Parents remember who helped, even if they don't join that day.

Pro tip

Send a one-page comparison sheet.

Blank columns for any two institutes: fee, batch size, doubt support, tests and results. Share it on WhatsApp.

Rule: never fill in the other institute's column yourself.

Your competitor-objection checklist

Save this for the next comparison call.

  • Never badmouth the other institute.
  • Ask what they liked about it.
  • Ask what matters most to them.
  • Compare on 4 things: batch, doubts, tests, results.
  • Don't drop your fee to match.
  • Send a blank comparison sheet on WhatsApp.
